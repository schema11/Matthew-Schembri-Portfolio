1 · Modelling approach

A serial arm is a coupled chain: each joint carries the weight and moment of everything distal to it. Both tabs model the worst-case pose — fully extended, straight, horizontal, gravity perpendicular to the links — and test three independent limits at every joint.

① Bending stress

Will the link snap? Compares applied moment against section modulus × allowable stress.

② Actuator torque

Can the motor hold the pose? Compares applied moment against peak torque × gear ratio × efficiency.

③ Tip deflection

Will it droop or wobble? Superposed cantilever deflection plus carried slope from every inboard link.

The true capability is the minimum of the three. For FDM-printed arms, ③ almost always governs long before ① does.

Governing equations

Moment demand at joint i, with dynamic amplification \(K_d\):

$$M_i = K_d\,g\left[P\,S_i + \sum_{j>i}\left(m_j\left(S_i-S_j+\tfrac{L_j}{2}\right) + P_j\left(S_i-S_j+L_j\right)\right)\right]$$

Capacity is the lesser of material and motor:

$$M_{cap,i} = \min\!\left(\underbrace{Z_i\,\frac{\sigma_{y,i}\,k}{\text{FoS}}}_{\text{bending}},\ \underbrace{T_{motor,i}\cdot i_{gear}\cdot \eta}_{\text{actuator}}\right)$$
